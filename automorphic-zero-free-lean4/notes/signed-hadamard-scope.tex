\paragraph{The signed Hadamard interchange.}
Let $\nu$ be a finite signed measure on the shift variable $z$.
Suppose the real Hadamard identity at the center and for
$|\nu|$-almost every $z$ takes the form
\[
 \sum_{\rho}K_\rho(z)=B(z)-D(z),\qquad
 K_\rho(z)=\frac{\sigma-\beta}
 {(\sigma-\beta)^2+(y+az-\gamma)^2}\ge0.
\]
Here the countable index includes multiplicity. If $B$ and $D$ are
integrable against the total variation $|\nu|$, then every partial
zero sum is dominated by the integrable right side. Integration of
the convergent series against the two Jordan parts and subtraction
of the center identity give
\[
 \int D\,d\nu-D(0)
  = \int B\,d\nu-B(0)
      -\sum_\rho\left(\int K_\rho\,d\nu-K_\rho(0)\right).
\]
The last series converges absolutely. Indeed
$|\int K_\rho\,d\nu|\le\int K_\rho\,d|\nu|$, and the sum of the
bounds is finite by monotone convergence. The module
\texttt{SignedHadamard} proves this statement using the signed
measure integral. Its arithmetic inputs are the original real
Hadamard identities and the total-variation integrability of the
gamma and logarithmic-derivative terms.
For each fixed zero at real separation $v>0$, the companion
\texttt{SignedPoisson} module identifies its signed average:
if $\nu$ is reflection-invariant and
$q(t)=\int\cos(tz)\,d\nu(z)$, then
\[
 \int \frac{v}{v^2+(y+az)^2}\,d\nu(z)
  =\int_0^\infty e^{-vx}\cos(yx)q(ax)\,dx.
\]
It verifies the sign of this actual signed integral at a retained zero
when the signed spectral law represents the prescribed tilt and the
prior strip reaches the zero. The far-zero module proves the separate
uniform estimate
\[
 \sum_{\rm far}|E_\rho|\le CM(1+D/\eta)^2
\]
from $|E_\rho|\le C/(L|z_\rho|^2)$, $|z_\rho|\ge\eta>0$,
$|z_{0,\rho}-z_\rho|\le D$ and
$\sum|z_{0,\rho}|^{-2}\le ML$ with absolute convergence. These
hypotheses leave the integrations by parts and the arithmetic
reference bound visible.
